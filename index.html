Is harness engineering dead for big models?

A common belief says it is basically dead. The papers say it is dead in seven places, alive in seven, and split in one.

2026-11-17, second edition. 870 of 874 papers read in full. 334 ideas judged.

Dead 7 places

✕ Dead

Raising the score on tasks the model already mostly completes

Top models gain about one point or nothing from a harness. Weaker models gain tens of points from the same one.

Rests on: The stronger the model and the higher its bare success, the smaller the harness gain: top models gain about one point or nothing, weaker models tens of points from the same harness.

Evidence: medium. 2611.15006

✕ Dead

Choosing among mature general harnesses

For score, it is dead. Swapping moves the score about as much as a rerun. It mostly moves cost, up to three times.

Rests on: With strong models, swapping among mature general coding harnesses moves score about as much as a rerun; it mostly moves token cost, up to three times; gaps shrink with more time.

Evidence: medium. 2611.16991

✕ Dead

Roles, delegation and parallel runs on a strong model

At equal budget they add nothing measurable over one plain agent session, and often cost more.

Rests on: With a strong model, specialist roles, model-chosen delegation, parallel runs and peer broadcast add nothing measurable over one plain agent session at equal budget, and often cost more.

Evidence: medium. 2610.40721

✕ Dead

The model checking its own work

Re-checking with no new outside information does not raise success. Sometimes it throws away correct work.

Rests on: A model told to re-check, reflect on or grade its own work, with no new outside information, does not raise success and sometimes discards correct work.

Evidence: medium. 2610.36405

✕ Dead

Adding a memory system

It does not reliably beat the built-in memory of the harness. The best added system differs by model.

Rests on: Adding a memory system to a harness does not reliably beat the harness's own built-in memory, and the best added system differs by model.

Evidence: medium. 2611.19359

✕ Dead

Automatic harness evolution where the bare model mostly succeeds

Held-out gains are zero to about five points, and the intervals include zero.

Rests on: When the starting harness is already strong and the bare model mostly succeeds, evolved edits add little: held-out gains of zero to about five points whose intervals include zero.

Evidence: medium. 2611.11994

✕ Dead

General skill libraries

They add little over a bare strong model. Only content matched to the task moves results.

Rests on: General skill libraries and broad guidance documents add little over a bare strong model; only content matched to the task moves results.

Evidence: medium. 2610.20673

Alive 7 places

✓ Alive

Tasks even the strongest model rarely finishes

A harness fitted to the task lifts even the strongest models by tens of points. Part of the jump is moving from one shot to any loop.

Rests on: A task-fitted harness (world model, code checks, repair loop) lifts even the strongest models by tens of points where they rarely finish bare; part of the jump is moving from one shot to any loop.

Evidence: medium. 2611.14949

✓ Alive

Knowledge the model lacks

Task-matched expert knowledge lifts success sharply when the model lacks it, such as a new method or a niche format.

Rests on: Task-matched expert knowledge placed in context (domain tutorials, method rules, procedural skills) lifts success sharply when the model lacks that knowledge, such as a new method or niche format.

Evidence: medium. 2611.14505

✓ Alive

Weaker and mid-strength models

A planning step lifts them by about 6 to 12 points. The gain varies by model.

Rests on: A written plan or planning step lifts weaker or mid-tier models by roughly 6 to 12 points on coding, retail and web tasks; the gain varies by model and is not guaranteed for each model.

Evidence: medium. 2610.15208

✓ Alive

Cost

With the model fixed, the harness changes cost per solved task by roughly 3 to 40 times.

Rests on: With the model fixed, swapping the harness changes cost per solved task by roughly 3 to 40 times, far more than tool interface or small prompt changes do.

Evidence: strong. 2610.33636

✓ Alive

Checking from outside

A separate critic, or a different coding product, raises accuracy at modest cost. Self-review does not.

Rests on: A separate critic or a different coding product, given the task and the changes, raises accuracy at modest extra cost; self-review at completion time does not.

Evidence: medium. 2610.38617

✓ Alive

Safety and permissions

Better models do not fix it. Out-of-authorization actions stay or rise as success rises.

Rests on: Stronger models are not safer: task success rises while out-of-authorization actions, drift and self-escalation stay or rise, and raising reasoning effort does not reduce them.

Evidence: medium. 2611.09113

✓ Alive

Measuring

A single run on tens of tasks cannot separate harnesses. Gaps of a few points are noise.

Rests on: A single run on tens of tasks cannot separate harnesses: swapping the harness flips about as many tasks as rerunning the same harness, so gaps of a few points are noise.

Evidence: strong. 2611.15268

◆ Alive up to a point

Reasoning effort

Alive up to the high setting on Claude models, dead beyond it. On GPT and Kimi models it already flattens above the middle setting.

Rests on: On strong Claude models, raising reasoning effort from low to medium or high buys large score gains; beyond high it flattens, and very low effort can even run slower.

Evidence: medium. 2611.05512

Rests on: Raising reasoning effort above the middle setting on GPT models, or from high to max on Kimi K3, multiplies time and tokens for little or no mean score change.

Evidence: medium. 2611.10600

So the papers point away from model size as the variable. What sorts the results is how often the bare model already finishes the task, and whether the harness supplies something the model cannot get alone.

Do not build on these

  • ✕
    Extra planning, review or role structure on a strong model leaving success flat. Contested. The surviving form is the roles verdict above. 2611.07461 contested
  • ✕
    One harness tuned on one model failing to carry over at all. The surviving form is a transfer with a smaller gain. 2611.11499 contested
  • ✕
    Mixing the work of different harnesses, such as a shared board of notes. Measured only before September, on one-run counts. 2609.40303 contested
  • ✕
    “Harness choice matters more than model choice among frontier models.” Model choice moves scores several times more. 2611.10600 superseded
  • ✕
    A fuller harness beating a minimal one by 8 to 13 points on a fixed model. Later papers measured far smaller gaps. 2610.26829 superseded
  • ✕
    A naive tool loop hurting a frontier model. It no longer does. 2610.37851 superseded
  • ✕
    Automatic harness evolution as a reliable lift. Held-out gains and transfer across models are not settled. 2610.41216 contested
  • ✕
    A harness beating equal-budget resampling. Gains often shrink or vanish at matched compute. Run the control. 2611.15754 contested
  • ✕
    A finish gate that checks outside evidence giving gains on strong models. Small gains, some null results. 2611.18359 contested
  • ✕
    Human gates at irreversible steps as a measured safety gain. Argued everywhere, never tested against no gate. 2610.19999 unmeasured

Design patterns from the papers

A pattern is a reusable design move found in the papers: one thing a harness does, such as summarising old history or checking a result outside the model. Each graph reads from the top. A column is one participant, an arrow is one step, and the numbered list says each step in words.

Consensus means what the field builds, not what works. The verdict comes from the separate table of ideas judged across papers, and a paper that “measured” a pattern mostly means its authors scored their own system. The graphs were drawn by a model from each pattern’s description and checked by script for form, not against the papers.

              What holds for every harness

              1. One run proves nothing. A single run on a few dozen tasks cannot separate harnesses. Run the same arm against itself first. That flip rate is the floor. 2611.15268
              2. Test on the exact model you ship. Which harness wins depends on the model. A gain on one model is not a gain on the next. 2611.01930
              3. Hold tasks out. A gain measured on the tasks that drove the search overstates the gain on unseen tasks. 2611.11342
              4. Audit the judge before trusting a number. Benchmark and grader defects move scores by tens of points. Run a reference solution through the grader. 2611.19359
              5. Compare against the bare model with the same budget. Retries, step limits, time limits and call counts explain a large part of reported gains. 2610.22466
              6. Count cost per solved task. Failed or looping runs use most tokens, so caps and early stops belong on the failure tail. 2610.32483
              7. The model does not check itself. Re-checking its own answer with no new outside information does not raise success, and sometimes throws away correct work. 2610.36405
              8. Guidance cuts both ways. Wrong guidance costs more than none. Only content matched to the task moves results. 2609.39578
              9. Parts do not add up. Gains from single parts do not sum to the full harness, and some parts lower scores. Add one, measure one. 2611.06116
              10. Every fix can break what worked. A model predicting its own regressions does little better than chance. Replay earlier cases before accepting a change. 2609.24130
              11. Rules live outside the conversation. Summaries lose rules and scope limits. Keep rules outside the conversation and put them back after every compaction. 2611.03223
              12. Authority is enforced by code outside the model. A check in code held on every model tested. A setting the model can bypass is not enforcement. 2611.13960
              13. Every write is safe to repeat. After a timeout, agents create duplicate side effects. A key on every write cut duplicates from 28 to 4 percent. 2609.29095

              How this was made, and where it stops

              The evidence base

              • 874 papers fetched between 1 March and 17 November 2026, all labelled from their abstracts.
              • 870 read in full. Of the other four, two were stopped by the safeguards of the model provider, one was never served by arXiv, and one was read in part.
              • 4,267 claims and 4,378 designs, each with a word-for-word quote. A script found the quote in its paper for 97 percent of the claims and 97 percent of the designs.
              • 539 design patterns, grouped from the designs.
              • 334 ideas, judged one topic at a time by a model: 269 survived, 42 contested, 14 stale, 6 unmeasured, 3 superseded.

              Read with care. “Helps” mixes three things: a higher score, a lower cost and better repeatability. Only claims tagged as measured on frontier models are counted. Claims marked not applicable are left out of the bars.

              Limits

              • Authors mostly score their own systems, so a gain they report is the least checked kind of evidence.
              • A language model judged each verdict, one topic at a time. A few verdicts flipped between two judging passes. Anything near a boundary, such as medium against weak or survived against contested, is a lead, not a fact.
              • The numbers come from tables that models built. A script checked the quotes (about 97 percent found). No person re-read the tables.
              • A quote found in its paper shows the sentence exists. Whether each quote supports the claim attached to it was not checked.
              • Many papers call strong open models “frontier”. Fewer measure the strongest closed models. The model tier of each claim is the reader’s judgment, not the paper’s.
              • November is a partial month.
              • Three "dead" verdicts (automatic harness evolution, choosing among general harnesses, roles and delegation) have contested neighbouring ideas in the same topics. Read them as the current weight of evidence.
